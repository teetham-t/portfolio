Oct 2026 – Present · MicroChain Token internship

Manifold: Photos and Manual to an Interactive 3D Guide

LLM agents · Blender · Image-to-3D

An AI pipeline that turns product photos and a PDF manual into a component-level 3D model with animated, step-by-step instructions.

5 image-to-3D / LLM models benchmarked · modeling Skill with 28 tests

  • Blender
  • Hunyuan3D
  • TRELLIS
Engineering summary

Problem

After-sales support needs to show people exactly which part to move and how. Building an accurate, animated 3D model of each product by hand is slow.

What I built

A build → check → review → repair loop around an LLM builder (Codex or Claude Code), with deterministic geometry and motion checks, a separate read-only reviewer and human approval before publishing. I packaged the modeling know-how as a reusable agent Skill for Blender (v1 → v3), compared 5 self-hosted image-to-3D and LLM models on an RTX 4090, and ran the pipeline on a 3-server Alibaba Cloud pilot.

Results so far

Real products (a pet feeder, a guitar, an office chair) built end to end in the cloud in 13–21 min. The v3 Skill passes 28 unit tests and catches every planted defect. Boxy products work well; curved products such as ergonomic chairs are still hard, and no result has yet been accepted for publication.

Next

Make the modeling Skill the main deliverable (fewer z-fighting and curved-surface artifacts, better materials, built-in power and door animations), document the pipeline, and explore higher-fidelity modeling for curved products.

Jun 2025 – May 2026 · Meticuly internship

Production CT Classification Pipeline

DICOM → 2D views → BiomedCLIP + EVA-02 → Quality ranking

Picks the best post-craniectomy skull scan from all the CT series in a case, for the production CraNeXt system.

99.4% body-region accuracy · 96.1% craniectomy accuracy

  • DICOM
  • NIfTI
  • BiomedCLIP
  • EVA-02
Engineering summary

Problem

Each case has many CT series: different planes, slice thicknesses, and bone or soft-tissue windows. The first version ranked them only with rules on DICOM metadata and folder names (gantry tilt, missing slices, slice thickness, bone window, pixel spacing, plane). Metadata cannot show what is in the image, so the top pick could be a skull scan without the craniectomy.

What I built

Converted DICOM to NIfTI and made axial, coronal and sagittal 2D views. BiomedCLIP labels the body region (skull/brain, skull and neck, neck, chest, abdomen). For skull scans, a fine-tuned EVA-02 classifies pre- or post-craniectomy, and the ranking now prefers post-craniectomy scans. The output is a ranked JSON file.

Model choice

Benchmarked 8 models (5M–1,012M parameters) on accuracy, speed and size, and chose the smallest, EVA-02 Tiny.

Results

99.4% body-region accuracy (321 of 323 predictions) and 96.1% craniectomy accuracy (99 of 103).

Meticuly internship

Cephalometric X-ray Landmark Detection

Detects 29 landmarks on cephalometric X-rays, built with dentists from Thammasat University. Compared 3 architectures on 1,000 images, using multi-GPU training on the LANTA Supercomputer.

1.12 mm mean radial error · 29 landmarks · 1,000 X-rays

  • PyTorch
  • Computer vision
  • Multi-GPU training
  • LANTA
Engineering summary

Problem

Orthodontists mark landmarks on side-view skull X-rays (cephalograms) by hand to plan treatment. It is slow and differs between people. An error under 2 mm is the usual clinical target.

What I built

Compared YOLOv11-pose, a hybrid Transformer-CNN (HTC) and NFDP (heatmaps with a learned prior), first on ISBI 2015 (400 images, 19 landmarks), then on the Aariz dataset (1,000 images from 7 X-ray machines, 29 landmarks). NFDP worked best, so I kept improving it: a spline-based version, different backbones (Swin, UMamba, SegResNet, MONAI Swin-UNETR) and contrast preprocessing (CLAHE).

Results

On Aariz, NFDP reached 1.14 mm with 86% of points within 2 mm (YOLO 1.88 mm, HTC 1.28 mm). Error analysis showed that one X-ray machine and a few landmarks caused most of the error; two expert annotators differed by about 0.49 mm on the same images. Later runs on the other six machines reached 1.04–1.12 mm.

What I learned

Benchmark scores do not carry over directly. On 20 in-house images with different landmark definitions, error was 2.54 mm (2.27 mm with CLAHE), so error analysis per landmark and per machine mattered more than one average number.

Feb 2023 – May 2025 · Team of 2 · C++ / Geant4

High-Energy Particle Simulation

Thailand Representative and Finalist, Regeneron ISEF 2025

Team project with a friend. We wrote a C++ simulation in CERN's Geant4 to study Relativistic Runaway Electron Avalanches under varying magnetic and intermediate fields, tracking particle trajectories and interactions at scale.

  • C++
  • Geant4
  • Simulation
Engineering summary

Question

Terrestrial gamma-ray flashes are bursts of gamma rays from thunderclouds, caused by runaway electrons that multiply in the cloud’s electric field (RREA). Earlier simulations assumed dry air. We asked how the cloud’s water content and the direction of Earth’s magnetic field change this process.

What we built

A Geant4 simulation in C++: a 10 km column of cloud with a downward electric field of 1,000–2,000 kV/m and a 1 MeV seed electron. We tested two cloud models (0–0.4% water mixed into air, and lower-density dry air for the cumulus and mature stages) and magnetic fields of 0–3,000 µT, parallel or perpendicular to the electric field. Python analysis fitted the growth length λ from electron counts at each height and measured photon energies.

Findings

Adding water to the air barely changed λ, but the lower-density mature-stage cloud lowered λ and the maximum photon energy (for example 39.0 to 32.0 MeV at 1,400 kV/m), which points to easier electrical breakdown. The magnetic field barely changed λ, but tilted the photon shower during the first ~500 ns.
